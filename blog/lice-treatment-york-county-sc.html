How Head Lice Spread in York County Schools

Lice don't jump or fly — they crawl from head to head during direct contact. That makes elementary school classrooms, after-school sports, sleepovers, and carpooling the most common transmission points. York County's fast-growing school districts — including Fort Mill School District 4, Clover School District, and Rock Hill School District 3 — have seen steady increases in enrollment, and more kids in close contact means more lice circulating in the community.

The tricky part is that lice are often present for 4–6 weeks before anyone notices. By the time a school sends home a lice notification, several families in the same carpool or friend group may already be affected. If your child's school has sent a notice, it's worth checking every head in the household — adult family members included.

Quick check: Part the hair at the nape of the neck and behind the ears — these are the warmest spots and where lice and nits are most concentrated. Nits (eggs) look like tiny white or tan specks glued tightly to the hair shaft, unlike dandruff which brushes away easily.

Frequently Asked Questions
Does my child have to miss school if they have lice?
Most York County and Fort Mill school districts follow a "no-nit" policy for returning to class. After your LCA appointment, we provide a written clearance letter confirming treatment is complete — most schools accept this for same-day return.
Do I need to bag up all our stuffed animals and wash every sheet?
No. Lice can only survive 24–48 hours off a human head. You don't need to do a whole-house deep clean. We give you a simple checklist: wash the pillowcases used the night before, put car headrests in a bag for two days, and do a quick vacuum of upholstered furniture. That's it.
